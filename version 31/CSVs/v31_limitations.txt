LIMITATIONS
- Statistical power: 5 day-out folds and [2] seed(s) per model -> paired fold tests have df = 4; a difference must be large and consistent across all days to be detectable.
- F1 vs D is a statistical tie: mission macro-F1 0.894 [0.832, 0.948] vs 0.893 [0.832, 0.948]; per-fold window F1 − D (seed-averaged-F1 method) mean +0.0015, F1 wins 3/5, p 0.90 [ONE METHOD ONLY (pooled-probability needs .npz)]. F1 is the headline for its interpretable, content-adaptive fusion, not for higher accuracy.
- F1 does NOT beat IMU alone: mission macro-F1 F1 0.894 [0.832, 0.948] vs I 0.968 [0.934, 1.000]; window 0.869 vs 0.893.
- Gate diagnostic: NOT SUPPORTED: across the 5 unseen days, F1's mean audio weight does not track which modality was stronger that day (Spearman -0.20, Pearson -0.16 with the A − I gap; with the F1 − I gap: Spearman -0.20, Pearson +0.06). The fold-level gate is also unstable across seeds (seed-to-seed SD 0.23 vs between-day SD 0.17), so a day's mean gate value is not a reproducible property of that day. With n = 5 folds, even a strong correlation would be weak evidence.
- These are leave-one-day-out results on 95 missions, not a held-out, never-touched final test set: many architectures were compared on these same folds over the project, so the best-looking numbers carry some selection optimism.
- Standard-split numbers reported earlier in the project (D and F1's predecessor architectures at ~0.89-0.91 macro-F1) used a random mission-level split whose test days also appear in training; they are NOT comparable to these day-out numbers and must not be cited alongside them without this caveat.
